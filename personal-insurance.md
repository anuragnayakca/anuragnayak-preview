Insurance is most useful when it is connected to a specific financial responsibility. A life-insurance conversation may begin with income replacement, debt or long-term family responsibilities. A disability-insurance conversation may begin with the financial effect of being unable to work. Critical illness coverage may be considered where a serious diagnosis could create costs, time away from work or other financial pressure.

The starting point should not be a predetermined coverage amount. It should be a review of what depends on your income, what resources are already available and how long those responsibilities are expected to continue.

## Life insurance

Term life insurance can be useful when a responsibility has a defined time horizon, such as a mortgage, dependent children or a period of high income-replacement need. Permanent life insurance is designed for longer-term coverage and may be considered for needs that do not disappear at a specific date. Permanent policies can also have additional features and higher funding requirements.

The choice between term and permanent coverage involves cost, duration, flexibility, estate objectives, cash-flow priorities and insurability. It should not be reduced to a rule that one type is always better.

## Critical illness insurance

A serious illness can affect income, time away from work, caregiving, treatment choices and household expenses. Critical illness insurance is intended to provide a benefit when the insured meets the policy definition of a covered condition and satisfies the contract requirements.

The details matter. Policies can differ in covered conditions, definitions, waiting periods, survival periods, exclusions, partial benefits and optional return-of-premium features. These should be reviewed using current insurer materials and the actual contract.

## Disability insurance

For someone whose financial plan depends heavily on earned income, an interruption to the ability to work may deserve particular attention. Workplace disability benefits can be valuable, but plan definitions, benefit amounts, maximums, waiting periods, taxable treatment and continuation after employment changes vary.

Personal disability coverage may offer different contract features or portability. The appropriate amount and structure depend on occupation, income, existing coverage, budget and underwriting.
