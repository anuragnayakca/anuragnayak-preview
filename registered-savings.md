## RRSP
Eligible RRSP contributions may generate a tax deduction. Growth is generally tax-deferred while funds remain in the plan, and withdrawals are generally taxable. Contribution room, current and future tax rates, employer plans and time horizon all matter.

## TFSA
TFSA contributions are made with after-tax dollars. Eligible growth and withdrawals are generally tax-free, and withdrawn amounts are generally added back to contribution room in a future year, subject to applicable rules.

## FHSA
For eligible first-time home buyers, an FHSA combines deductible contributions with qualifying tax-free withdrawals for a first home. Eligibility and contribution rules should be reviewed before opening or funding an account.

## RESP
RESPs are designed for education savings and may qualify for government grants depending on eligibility and contribution history. Family circumstances, beneficiary age and withdrawal rules are important considerations.

## Investment options within your plan
The account type and the investment held inside it are separate decisions. Depending on licensing, objectives and suitability, available investment options may include insurance-based investment contracts such as segregated funds. Features, guarantees, fees, legal treatment and suitability vary by contract and individual circumstances.

## Common questions about registered savings
### RRSP or TFSA first?
The answer depends on current and expected future tax rates, employer matching, contribution room, liquidity needs and the goal for the money. A tax deduction today is useful only in the context of the broader plan.

### Who can use an FHSA?
FHSA eligibility is governed by federal rules, including first-time home buyer requirements. Contribution limits and qualifying withdrawal rules should be checked for the individual's circumstances before contributing.

### How do RESP grants fit into the decision?
Eligible RESP contributions may attract government grants, subject to program rules, beneficiary age and contribution history. Catch-up opportunities can exist, but they should be reviewed against the family's cash-flow priorities.

### Are segregated funds the same thing as an RRSP or TFSA?
No. An RRSP or TFSA is an account or plan type. A segregated fund is an insurance contract that can, where available and appropriate, be held within certain registered or non-registered structures. Contract guarantees, fees and legal features vary.

### Should registered savings be reviewed when income changes?
Yes. A material income change, new employer plan, home purchase, new child or transition to self-employment can change which account deserves priority and how much liquidity should remain outside long-term savings.
