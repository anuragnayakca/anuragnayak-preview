## Key-Person Protection
The death, disability or serious illness of an owner or critical employee can affect revenue, client relationships, financing and day-to-day operations. Insurance may be one tool used to provide liquidity while the business adjusts, subject to policy terms and the needs being addressed.

## Buy-Sell Funding
A shareholder or buy-sell agreement can establish what should happen after certain triggering events. Life insurance may be one potential funding tool for a death-related obligation. The legal agreement, business valuation and insurance structure should be coordinated with qualified legal, tax and accounting professionals where appropriate.

## Corporate-Owned Life Insurance
Corporate-owned life insurance may be considered for certain business protection, estate or succession objectives. Depending on the policy structure and circumstances, insurance proceeds may also affect the corporation's Capital Dividend Account.

Corporate insurance strategies should be coordinated with qualified tax and legal professionals. Tax treatment and the amount of any Capital Dividend Account credit depend on the facts and policy structure.

## Business Continuity
Continuity planning can include ownership transition, succession, financial obligations and the impact on family members, shareholders, employees and clients. Insurance is one possible funding component, not the entire continuity plan.

## Personal Protection for Owners
The owner may be central to both the business and the household's financial picture. Personal life, critical illness and disability coverage can therefore deserve a separate review alongside the business plan.

## Common questions about business protection
### Who is a key person?
A key person is someone whose death, disability or prolonged absence could materially affect revenue, operations, financing, client relationships or specialized knowledge. It may be an owner, executive, salesperson or technical employee depending on the business.

### How is buy-sell insurance connected to the shareholder agreement?
The agreement defines the legal process and obligations; insurance can be one funding source for certain events. Coverage should be coordinated with the agreement and a current valuation rather than selected independently.

### Does corporate-owned life insurance always create a full CDA credit?
No. The Capital Dividend Account treatment depends on the death benefit, adjusted cost basis and corporate circumstances at the time. Tax and legal professionals should be involved in the structure and eventual distribution strategy.

### Can a business insure disability or critical illness risks too?
Depending on the need and available products, disability, critical illness or business-overhead coverage may be considered for certain risks. The appropriate owner, beneficiary, amount and purpose should be clearly defined before selecting coverage.

### Is group benefits part of business protection?
It addresses a different problem, but it belongs in the wider conversation. Employee benefits can support recruitment, retention and employee wellbeing, while key-person and shareholder strategies address continuity and ownership risks.
