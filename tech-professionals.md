## Start with your workplace plan
Before considering anything new, understand the life, disability, health and optional coverage already available through your employer. Coverage amounts, definitions, taxation and what happens when employment ends can all matter.

## Career changes deserve a benefits review
A promotion, job change, layoff, move to consulting or transition into an incorporated role can change more than compensation. Employer-provided coverage may change or end, while personal responsibilities continue.

## Personal protection
Life, critical illness and disability insurance can address different risks. The question is not whether everyone needs every type of coverage. It is whether your existing benefits, personal resources and responsibilities leave anything worth reviewing.

## Registered savings
RRSP, TFSA, FHSA and RESP accounts serve different purposes. Income, employer programs, contribution room, family goals and time horizon should guide how they fit together.

## Incorporated and contract professionals
Moving from employment to contracting or incorporation can introduce additional questions about income protection, business continuity and corporate insurance. Personal and business considerations should be reviewed separately, then connected where appropriate.

## Questions tech professionals often ask
### Are workplace benefits enough on their own?
Sometimes they may be. The useful question is whether the amounts, definitions and duration of your workplace coverage line up with your income, debts, dependants and savings. A review can confirm that your current setup is appropriate or identify a specific area worth examining.

### What should I check before changing jobs?
Look at when your current life, disability, health and dental coverage ends, when the new plan begins, and whether there is a waiting period. Also review any optional coverage, conversion privileges and employer savings programs so you understand what is changing before the transition happens.

### What changes when I move from employee to contractor?
Contractors may no longer have employer-paid benefits, disability coverage or matching programs. Income can also become less predictable. That does not automatically mean buying multiple new policies; it means rebuilding the picture around the benefits and resources that remain.

### Why does the definition of disability matter?
Disability policies can define when a person is considered disabled in different ways. Waiting periods, benefit periods, maximum benefits and occupational definitions can materially affect how coverage responds. Those details deserve as much attention as the monthly benefit amount.

### RRSP or TFSA first?
There is no universal order. Income, marginal tax rate, employer matching, available contribution room, liquidity needs and the purpose of the money all matter. The account should follow the objective rather than a generic rule.
