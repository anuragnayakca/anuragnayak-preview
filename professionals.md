Many professionals spend years building skills, accepting greater responsibility and improving their income. Along the way, workplace benefits, personal insurance and investment accounts are often established at different times and for different reasons.

A good income and regular saving are valuable, but they do not automatically answer questions such as whether income risk is adequately addressed, whether workplace coverage would continue after a job change, or whether account choices still fit current goals and access needs.

## Career changes can alter more than salary

A new employer can change life insurance, disability benefits, health and dental coverage, pension or group-savings arrangements and other workplace programs. A move into consulting or incorporated work can remove employer-sponsored benefits altogether and create new decisions around income protection and business obligations.

The goal of a review is not to assume that your workplace plan is inadequate. Workplace benefits can be a valuable foundation. The question is what the plan actually provides, what happens when employment changes and whether there are responsibilities that sit outside the plan.

## Multiple accounts can serve different purposes

RRSPs, TFSAs, FHSAs and other account structures are not interchangeable. Eligibility, tax treatment, contribution rules, access to funds and time horizon can differ. The discussion should begin with the purpose of the money and the flexibility you may need, not with a favourite account type.

## Income protection deserves its own review

For many professionals, future earning ability is one of the largest financial resources supporting a mortgage, family, savings plan or business. Disability coverage can be reviewed in relation to occupation, income, workplace benefits, definitions, waiting periods and benefit periods. No single policy structure is appropriate for everyone.
