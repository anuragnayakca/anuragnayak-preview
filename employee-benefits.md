Employee benefits are often discussed as a recruiting tool, but that is only part of the decision. A benefit plan is a recurring business commitment that should make sense for the employees who use it and for the employer who funds or administers it.

A good review therefore begins with workforce needs, business size, budget, employee participation and the existing plan, if one is already in place.

## Health and dental coverage

Extended health and dental benefits can help employees with eligible expenses that are not fully covered by provincial programs. Plans differ in deductibles, reimbursement percentages, annual or lifetime maximums, practitioner categories, prescription-drug rules and dental schedules.

Those details matter more than a simple statement that a plan includes "health and dental."

## Life and disability benefits

Group life and disability coverage can provide important employee protection, but the benefit amount, eligibility, definitions, waiting periods and continuation after employment changes vary by plan.

Employers should understand both the employee value and the administrative obligations connected to the benefit.

## Reviewing an existing plan

A plan that worked when a business had five employees may not fit in the same way after growth, changes in workforce demographics or rising claims. Review does not automatically mean replacing the provider. It can also mean clarifying plan features, adjusting design or confirming that the existing arrangement remains appropriate.
