Non-registered investing can be useful because it is not limited by the same contribution-room rules as registered accounts. That flexibility comes with a different tax profile and requires careful attention to the purpose of the money and the investment selected.

The question is not simply whether a non-registered account is available. It is whether the additional flexibility is useful for the goal and whether the investor understands the potential tax and risk implications.

## Liquidity and time horizon

Money that may be needed soon should not automatically be invested the same way as money intended for a long-term goal. The discussion should identify when funds may be required, what level of fluctuation is acceptable and how much emergency liquidity should remain outside longer-term investments.

## Tax considerations

Interest, dividends and realized capital gains can be taxed differently. Account ownership and investment type can also matter. General educational explanations can help frame the discussion, but personal tax conclusions should be reviewed with a qualified tax professional.

## Investment selection still requires suitability

An account is only a container. The investment inside it must still reflect objectives, time horizon, risk tolerance, capacity for loss and product-specific features. Performance projections or promises should not drive the decision.
