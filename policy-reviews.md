Insurance policies are often purchased at a specific moment: a mortgage, marriage, birth of a child, business loan, partnership or job change. Years later, the policy may still be in force while the reason for buying it is less clear.

A policy review should answer two separate questions. First, what does the existing contract actually provide? Second, does that coverage still fit the responsibility it was intended to support?

## Understand the contract before comparing alternatives

Important features can include coverage amount, term, renewal schedule, conversion privileges, premium structure, riders, exclusions, ownership and beneficiaries. Permanent policies may also have guarantees, cash values or other contract features that require careful review.

Comparing only the premium can miss important differences.

## Changes do not automatically require replacement

A larger mortgage, higher income or new family responsibility may justify a review, but a review does not automatically mean additional insurance. Likewise, an older policy with a higher renewal premium may still have valuable features or conversion options.

If a replacement is considered, underwriting, new contestability periods, exclusions, policy features and the risk of losing existing coverage all need attention.

> **Important:** Do not cancel an existing policy until any new coverage has been approved, accepted, paid for and confirmed in force.
