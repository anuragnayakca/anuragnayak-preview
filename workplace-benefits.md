## Start with the benefits booklet, not a new policy
A useful review begins by identifying what your employer already provides. That may include life insurance, accidental death coverage, short-term disability, long-term disability, health and dental benefits, and optional employee-paid coverage.

## Questions worth asking
- How much life insurance is provided, and is it tied to salary?
- How is disability income calculated and taxed?
- Are there waiting periods, benefit maximums or occupational definitions?
- Which benefits end when employment ends?
- Can any coverage be converted or continued?
- Does your current coverage reflect your mortgage, dependants and other responsibilities?

## When changing jobs
A job change can affect more than salary. Employer life and disability benefits may end, reset or change. Reviewing the transition before the old coverage disappears can make the change easier to understand.

## When your workplace coverage may already be enough
Additional personal insurance is not automatically necessary. If existing benefits and personal resources appropriately address your needs, confirming that can be a useful outcome too.

## Common questions about workplace benefits
### What usually happens to benefits when I leave a job?
Many employer-provided benefits end when employment ends, although timing and continuation rights vary by plan. Some life or health benefits may have conversion or continuation options with deadlines, so the plan documents should be checked promptly.

### Why does the tax treatment of disability benefits matter?
Who pays the premium can affect how disability benefits may be taxed. The details depend on the plan structure and applicable tax rules, so it is worth confirming the arrangement rather than assuming the stated monthly benefit equals take-home income.

### Should I buy optional life insurance through work?
Optional workplace coverage may be convenient and competitively priced, but portability, future pricing, underwriting and what happens when employment changes are worth comparing with personally owned alternatives.

### What documents should I bring to a review?
A benefits booklet or summary, recent benefit statements, information about optional coverage and any existing personal policies are a practical starting point. You do not need to submit policy numbers or medical details through the public website form.

### Can the outcome of a review be to make no changes?
Yes. If the current benefits, personal coverage and financial resources appropriately address the responsibilities being reviewed, confirming that can be the right outcome.
